# Creative and product decisions

- **A field guide, not a winner board.** The audience starts with an evening, a friend and a question. The story gives them a reason to care before opening the planner.
- **Original dusk illustrations.** Amber Hong Kong dining streets and teal Shenzhen avenues establish one coherent world. Generated original scenes plus a lightweight original SVG are documented in `public/art/README.md`. The scenes are explicitly imagined settings, separate from the sourced transport schematic.
- **A tangible decision loop.** Notice a possibility → set a priority → consider the return trip → compare → keep a field note → revise or replay. Narrative language is authored and does not pretend to read the visitor’s mind.
- **An honest preference rubric.** Money, time, eligibility and personal priorities remain legible. The logic is deterministic, separate from React, and tested with missing data and pathological inputs.
- **Calm research workspace.** Business questions live in a separate evidence desk. It is visibly an early research scaffold, not a fabricated market dataset or a fake chat service.
- **No financial magic.** The surplus lab conserves one hypothetical fixed pool. It never connects ownership to automatic extra productivity or investment returns.
- **Portable but maintainable.** The normal app uses typed modules and a static production build. A separate export script embeds that exact build and original assets for private offline delivery. The export is not claimed tested until actually rendered from `file://`.
- **Low ongoing network need.** No runtime API, tracking, account, map tiles, video or audio. The three day scenes, two geometry-matched night variants and transparent portrait total about 3 MB before bundling. Fonts are self-hosted, including a renamed 7.3 KB OFL CJK subset for the eight Chinese interface characters so these labels do not depend on operating-system fonts. Network and media failures have readable fallbacks.
- **Reference discipline.** Pixel Shanghai informed cohesion, spatial continuity and a complete replayable loop, not copied code or assets. Supahero, Seesaw, Saaspo and SearchSystem are additional craft references, not endorsed claims or dependencies.

## Playable-evening pass

The baseline’s world was visually coherent but its choices were too close to a guided form. The new loop lets the player inspect a table, a street and a return route for free; commits visibly advance a story clock through the same tested engine. One disclosed delay asks what to preserve, without portraying a longer walk or either city as morally better. Jun provides continuity through an original adult portrait and a sketchbook motive, not an approval meter. Late and over-budget endings remain clearly tentative.

A compact setup disclosure keeps the scene prominent on phones. The return deadline and per-person budget stay visible, with a full-outing estimate distinct from money already spent. The two night variants preserve the original scene geometry; text and hit targets remain DOM elements. All art has static fallbacks and no sound is required.

The previous fixed 55/85 discovery values are removed. Explicit activity familiarity can inform the optional practical rubric, while the story shows choices and consequences without a city winner.

## Scene-first refinement

Actual browser captures revealed that a stacked heading, setup, progress and status preamble pushed the world below the first viewport. Active play now aims for a compact navigation and status line followed immediately by the contained scene, named inspections and current conversation/choice. Detailed setup, familiarity, sources and route calculations remain reachable without competing with the current decision. Long warnings and enlarged text can scroll naturally rather than being clipped to meet a screenshot target.

The known-green six-beat state and calculation contracts stay unchanged in this pass. A browser-recorded walkthrough will review pacing and focus alongside viewport screenshots; this is evidence of interaction, not a mock animation.

## World as the interface

Audience feedback overruled the smaller-sidebar direction: a world surrounded by planning panels still felt like a website, and asking someone to keep a memento introduced an unwanted sense of completion. The new primary entry is the place itself. A phone, menu and wallet can reveal a question or a consequence; brief conversations then return control to free exploration. Only explicit travel, meal and walk commitments move the engine’s clock.

The palette follows the original scenes, with restrained neutral dialogue rather than a universal green shell. No extra avatar movement framework is needed. A wallet shows an illustrative spending allowance and projected bill, not cash on hand or a connected bank balance. The human evening remains suitable for adult professional audiences without inventing a supplier meeting or a business-trip history. Both fictional adults still start from the same modeled origin.

No memento selection, collection count, streak, mandatory journal or reward appears in play. Quiet technical resume remains and existing saved notes are preserved. Business research and the practical planner are available on request; opening an existing plan is distinct from explicitly copying story choices into it.
