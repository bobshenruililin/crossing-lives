# A ten-minute Fellows presenter path

One audience decision, one complete evening, one business question. This uses the same story and decision engine as ordinary play. The timings below are rehearsal allocations, not a measured presentation or evidence of audience understanding. There is no separate guided engine, autoplay or forward chapter-jump system.

**Provocation:** A lower personal bill can come with a longer journey. It does not tell us who has a voice in the business; that needs evidence about rights.

## Before presenting

Use the exact verified portable or static build identified in the verification record. The complete experience works without an account, sound or network; opening external source links needs connectivity.

For an edition whose verification record covers temporary mode, use **More → Start temporary demo**. The new tab is titled **Between · Temporary demo** and explicitly says changes will not be saved. Keep the original tab open; close only the temporary tab when finished. Reloading that temporary page starts over.

Until that mode's exact browser gate is verified, or for an older edition without the link, use a dedicated clean demo browser profile. Merely opening another normal tab on the same origin does not isolate saves. Do not reset a personal story as a harmless presentation shortcut: **Start a fresh evening → Start a fresh story** replaces both saved story attempts, including older notes. Reset only a disposable demo context.

In that profile, open **More (Story options) → Evening setup**. Confirm **Room to wander**: two fictional adults at the same Kowloon/Tsim Sha Tsui origin, departure **16:30**, home deadline **23:30**, allowance **HK$400/person**, and the disclosed **30-minute dinner delay** enabled. Use **Rail via Lo Wu** for the Shenzhen preview. Keep the simple dinner and 45-minute walk the same in both forecasts.

Begin with Jun’s invitation visible. His memories belong to him; the player need not share his biography or familiarity with either city.

## 0:00–0:45 · One ordinary evening

Leave the invitation over the world. Say once:

“Two fictional adults are choosing dinner and a walk from the same Hong Kong starting point. The dialogue, prices, exchange rate and travel buffers are examples. Published border hours have sources. This demo checks no one’s immigration eligibility.”

Frame one audience decision: stay nearby or cross for dinner. Do not introduce the whole feature set or ask for several preference rankings.

## 0:45–2:30 · See the tradeoff before choosing

1. **Look around first → Wallet.** Before choosing a city, the whole-outing estimates are **Hong Kong HK$304.00/person** and **Shenzhen HK$260.22/person**. They include getting home. **Put the wallet away.**
2. **Look at Hong Kong → Phone.** Show the conditional **modeled home 19:15**. **Put the phone away.**
3. **Look at Shenzhen → Phone.** Keep **Rail via Lo Wu**. Show **modeled home 22:15**. Open **Evening assumptions** once: **60-minute simple dinner, 45-minute walk, disclosed 30-minute dinner delay**. The actual clock is still **16:30**. Close the fold and **Put the phone away.**

Say: “For these example inputs, crossing costs HK$43.78 less per person and adds three hours of round-trip travel. That comparison includes returning home.” Local travel is 15 minutes each way; the rail scenario is 1 hour 45 minutes each way, including authored border/local allowances.

This is scenario arithmetic, not a market finding, guaranteed journey time or monetary value assigned to time or belonging. Keep attention on spending, then home time, rather than every line item.

## 2:30–3:00 · One audience decision

Ask: **“Given those assumptions, shall we stay nearby or cross for dinner?”** Take one show of hands.

If Hong Kong wins, choose **Look at Hong Kong**; otherwise stay in the Shenzhen preview. Select **Talk about dinner**. Keep the dinner simple and the longer walk fixed for this demonstration instead of adding more audience votes.

## 3:00–5:30 · Let that choice change the world

- Hong Kong: **Head to the table**. Shenzhen: **Head to the station**.
- **Read the menu → Let’s keep dinner simple.**
- **Step outside → Let’s take the longer walk.**
- **Look around.** Pause on the night scene. Let the place and Jun’s short acknowledgment do some of the work.

Reference values for the operator:

| Moment | Hong Kong | Shenzhen, rail |
|---|---:|---:|
| Arrive at table | 16:45 | 18:15 |
| Simple dinner and once-only 30-minute delay finish | 18:15 | 19:45 |
| 45-minute walk finishes | 19:00 | 20:30 |
| Whole outing per person | HK$304.00 | HK$260.22 |
| Modeled home | 19:15 | 22:15 |

These values are derived from the existing story/route selectors. Neither branch is the correct personal answer. The default scenario leaves time in both; do not manufacture urgency or imply that everyone can actually cross.

## 5:30–6:30 · Finish the journey, including home

**Head home → Check the return route.** Point to **Coming home**: **19:00–19:15** locally, or **20:30–22:15** via Lo Wu. The detailed legs are optional.

**Back to Jun → Follow the return journey → Finish the evening.** Arrive in the shared fictional Hong Kong home scene. In the Shenzhen branch, modeled clearance-hour fit still leaves actual entry, services, seats and queues unverified.

The optional fictional phone message and replay can be left for later. Neither is required to complete this argument.

## 6:30–9:15 · A spending choice opens a rights question

At home, **Wallet → Who benefits from this spending?** The context is the completed outing: **HK$608.00 for two** locally, or **HK$520.44 for two** by rail. It includes transport; it is not one restaurant’s revenue or verified profit.

Open **What does membership let you do? → Worker decisions**. Choose only this question; do not read the default customer-access answer and then tour every topic. The question is **“What decision rights do workers have?”**

- Home Market’s reviewed pages describe customer benefits but do not establish worker voting rights or ownership. Undisclosed here does not mean absent.
- Cheese Board’s About page describes equal worker ownership/control. The modified-consensus meeting detail and candidacy rules come from a **historical 2022 notice**; current terms and candidates’ precise rights are unverified.
- These organizations are in **Hong Kong and Berkeley**, not the fictional restaurants and not a Hong Kong–Shenzhen financial comparison. Rights descriptions do not measure comparative returns.

Keep the adjacent source links and limits visible. Do not open the entire source ledger, business scaffold, roadmap or fixed-pool exercise during this path. If asked afterward, the allocation exercise is a separate hypothetical amount; customer savings are not treated as business profit and ownership does not create extra returns in the model.

## 9:15–10:00 · One takeaway

“Lower personal spending can come with a longer journey. It does not tell us who has a voice in the business; that requires evidence about rights.”

This connects individual choice, the complete crossing tradeoff and different membership rights. It does not claim that crossing changes a business’s governance. End with discussion rather than a catalogue of remaining features.

## Optional exploration after the talk

**More → Open my plan** opens the independent practical plan without copying story inputs into it. The Wallet’s planner-import control instead replaces that plan’s inputs after confirmation; cancel if those inputs should be kept.

The planner uses the active story choices and recalculates the other city from starting assumptions. It does **not** compare two preserved completed playthroughs. In particular, a previous Hong Kong lingering-dinner/long-walk ending is not automatically the Hong Kong card beside a later Shenzhen simple-dinner/short-walk plan. Do not call those cards “the two evenings we just played.”

The story’s explicit rewind and other-evening controls remain useful for optional exploration. Free observations, city previews and object views do not advance time. Each travel, meal, walk and return commitment uses the same guarded engine.

## Rehearsal and evidence limits

Rehearse both audience outcomes in the isolated demo context. Check exact controls and outcomes, projector readability, returning to the scene, and reaching the single rights question without traversing the research library. Automated interaction and screenshot checks can establish those behaviors; they cannot establish a ten-minute human pace or audience comprehension.
